Real Algorithms: Grover & Shor

Two of the most famous quantum algorithms — one we'll actually run today, one we'll only admire from a distance.

Grover's Search: Finding a Needle Faster

Imagine an unsorted phone book with a million entries and you need to find the one entry matching a phone number. Classically, in the worst case, you have to check entries one by one — up to a million checks. Grover's algorithm finds it in roughly the square root of that many steps — about a thousand instead of a million. That's a quadratic speedup, and it applies to any "needle in a haystack" search problem with no special structure to exploit.

How? Amplitude Amplification

Grover's trick is to put all possible answers into superposition at once, then repeatedly nudge the "correct" answer's probability amplitude up while nudging the wrong answers' amplitudes down. After enough nudges (roughly √N of them), measuring the qubits gives you the right answer with very high probability.

Shor's Algorithm: Why It Worries Cryptographers

Factoring a large number into its prime components is easy to state and brutally slow classically — the best known classical algorithms take exponential-ish time as numbers get bigger, which is exactly why RSA encryption (used all over the internet) relies on factoring being hard.

Shor's algorithm factors large numbers in polynomial time on a sufficiently large, fault-tolerant quantum computer. If such a machine existed at scale, it could break RSA encryption as we know it. That "if" is doing a lot of work today — current quantum hardware isn't remotely large or reliable enough to run Shor's algorithm against real-world key sizes — but it's exactly why "post-quantum cryptography" is already an active research and standardization effort.

We won't implement Shor's today. It requires far more qubits and error correction than a workshop laptop simulator can practically demonstrate. Think of it as inspiration for where the field is headed, not something on today's hands-on menu.
Grover's, though, we can run. Chapter 7 walks through a full 2-qubit Grover's search you'll build yourself.
