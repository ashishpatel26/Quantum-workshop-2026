Where to Go Next

You built and ran real quantum circuits today — superposition, entanglement, a search algorithm, and a working quantum RNG. That knowledge transfers directly beyond the simulator.

From Simulator to Real Hardware

Everything you wrote today runs on real IBM Quantum hardware with minimal changes. The AerSimulator we used behaves like a quantum computer; swapping it for a real quantum backend uses the same QuantumCircuit objects, the same gates, and largely the same Qiskit API. The concepts, code structure, and intuition you built today are not simulator-only tricks — they are the actual skills used to program real quantum processors.

Where to Keep Learning

Qiskit Global Summer School

IBM's free, in-depth annual summer school covering quantum computing from fundamentals through advanced algorithms, with lecture recordings and hands-on labs available afterward.

IBM Quantum Learning

IBM's structured learning platform with courses ranging from "Basics of Quantum Information" to advanced algorithm design, plus access to run circuits on real IBM Quantum hardware.

Qiskit Textbook

A comprehensive, freely available textbook covering the math, code, and intuition behind quantum computing and Qiskit in much greater depth than a single workshop can.

Ideas to Keep Building

  • Extend today's Grover's search to 3+ qubits and multiple marked states.
  • Explore quantum teleportation — another classic Bell-pair-based protocol.
  • Try running one of today's circuits on real IBM Quantum hardware and compare the (noisier!) results to the simulator.
  • Look into variational algorithms (VQE, QAOA) — today's near-term approach to quantum advantage on imperfect hardware.
